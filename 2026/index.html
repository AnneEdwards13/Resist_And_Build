    Venues

    Main Venue: Tivoli Turnhalle, 900 Auraria Pkwy, Denver, Colorado

    Much of the Summit will take place in the Tivoli Turnhalle at the Auraria Campus on the second floor. The Tivoli Student Union is at the Auraria Campus in downtown Denver, located on the traditional territories and ancestral homelands of the Cheyenne and Arapahoe Nations. The Auraria campus is home to three institutions: the University of Colorado Denver (CU Denver), Metropolitan State University of Denver (MSU Denver), and the Community College of Denver (CCD).

    Getting there: Public transit options are available for those nearby and can be found here.

    Parking: There are lots of parking garage options and details about parking on the online map here.

    Concurrent Workshops will be held in one of several locations at the Tivoli Student Union building: Adirondacks (No. 440), Zenith (No. 640), LoRaine Good (No. 444), and the Baerresen Ballroom (320A-C). See the map of the Tivoli Student Union here.

    Kick-Off Venue: Colorado People's Center, 730 21st St Denver, Colorado

    The Colorado People's Center is a volunteer-run shared resource space where people can gather, organize, learn from one another, and build relationships. The Colorado People’s Center is located at 730 21st St. in Denver. This venue is located on the land of the hinono’eino’ biito’owu’ (Arapaho), Núu-agha-tʉvʉ-pʉ̱ (Ute), and the Očhéthi Šakówiŋ and Tsétsėhéstȧhese/Tsitsistas/Só’taeo’o (Cheyenne) Nations.

    Getting there: The Colorado People’s Center is about a 30-minute walk from the main venue and a 10-minute walk from the 18th St / Stout Station stop.

    Who’s Organizing the Summit?

    The national Summit Planning Circle includes representatives from the US Solidarity Economy Network, Build & Fight, Art.coop, People’s Network for Land and Liberation, Next System Studies, and The Democracy Collaborative.

    Strategic Objectives

    Movement Priorities and Goals

    In the current context, our priorities are to:

    • Strengthen networks and trust across the solidarity economy and systems change movements, particularly centering leadership from Southern, Black, Indigenous, and marginalized communities globally.
    • Enrich and expand our movements, making them irresistible by fostering broad alignment around systemic change. Build alliances that overcome fragmentation, moving together on common ground to scale movements toward transformative change.
    • Deepen our collective vision and strategies for liberation from the interlocking systems of oppression, extraction, and domination. This includes cultivating shared strategies to address capitalist exploitation, colonialism, ecological harm, and inequality, with the ultimate goal of achieving systemic transformation.
    • Draw from the visionary traditions of BIPOC and other oppressed and exploited peoples to fight for freedom, create community-driven solutions, and reconnect with the land and nonhuman kin, which offer essential inspiration for building the regenerative, just futures we seek.
    • Foster a joyful, regenerative culture that embodies the values of justice, interdependence, and ecological restoration, emphasizing care, mutual aid, and collective healing. Draw insights from feminisms, disability justice, and solidarity frameworks to prioritize ongoing individual and collective well-being.

    Collaborative Planning Approach

    A joint effort between the local host circle, national Backbone Circle, and Summit Planning Circle focusing on key collaboration areas:

    • Venue selection and logistics
    • Local outreach and attendee engagement
    • Program development
    • “Carnival Beyond Capitalism” festival
    • Volunteer and vendor coordination

    The national team provides support and guidance, while the local committee offers Atlanta-specific insights and networks.

    Read “Our Task Ahead: Reclaiming Revolutionary Struggle in Atlanta and the South“, by Jasmine Banks and April Doner, Nonprofit Quarterly
